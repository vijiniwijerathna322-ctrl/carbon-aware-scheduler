# From hourly grid observations to reviewable scheduling decisions

## The problem I chose

A flexible load has two decisions: how much energy it needs and when that energy should be used. This project concentrates on the second decision. If a job can finish at several different times before its deadline, a forecast can rank those alternatives. That creates a practical connection between a time-series model and an optimizer, rather than treating predictive accuracy as the end of the exercise. The central question is whether a forecast-based schedule performs better than immediate execution when later observations are used to score it.

I deliberately kept the workload illustrative. The replay uses constant-power EV-style jobs with a small set of durations and deadline windows. It does not represent a measured household or industrial fleet. This makes the decision rules and comparisons reproducible, while limiting the environmental claims I can make. A project that estimates real avoided emissions would also need defensible marginal-emissions data, customer constraints and representative workloads. Those are future extensions rather than assumptions hidden behind the reported savings.

## Data and the physical meaning of the target

The hourly source is EIA-930 balancing-authority data. I use observed demand and fuel generation to construct separate demand and carbon targets. The carbon target is a generation-weighted lifecycle proxy over covered technologies. The factor configuration records IPCC AR5 technology medians and a separately attributed historical UK oil proxy. The resulting unit is grams of carbon-dioxide equivalent per kilowatt-hour. Negative generation does not contribute negative emissions to the weighted mix, and unresolved other fuels and storage categories are excluded rather than assigned arbitrary factors.

This definition matters as much as the forecasting method. The proxy ignores imports and does not reconstruct storage charging history. A partially observed fuel mix can be different from the complete mix that actually supplied consumption. Average generation intensity is also different from the marginal response to adding or moving a load. I therefore describe replay outcomes as proxy reductions. The datasheet records coverage and missingness diagnostics so that an attractive optimization result cannot erase uncertainty in the physical target itself.

The measured CISO snapshot contains 50,646 hourly rows from the beginning of 2021 through October 2026. The quality report distinguishes missing cells, rows with remaining gaps and structural column history. For example, geothermal observations begin much later than the rest of the history. Treating that missing column history as an outage of the entire grid would be misleading. Raw forecasting inputs remain pointwise and retain gaps; retrospective interpolation and centered spike filters are reserved for explicitly labelled processed-data exploration.

## Forecasting with information boundaries

Each target uses a global LightGBM quantile model with the forecast horizon as a feature. This shares statistical strength across the next 48 hours without maintaining a separate model artifact for every horizon. Features include issue-time lags, trailing summaries, calendar information and seasonal anchors whose timestamps are constrained by the issue time. The implementation physically passes only the historical prefix to prediction. This is a stronger boundary than asking a feature function to remember which rows should be ignored.

Evaluation follows chronological rolling-origin refits. Daily region-local midnight issues produce future forecasts, and realized labels are attached only after prediction. Models are periodically refitted using earlier observations. Missing labels are not filled with convenient zeros, and comparison tables retain finite-sample counts. Paired comparisons align issue time, target time and horizon before computing differences. An additional source limitation remains: the API snapshot contains latest revisions, rather than a complete archive of the observations available to an operator at each historical issue. The replay consequently assumes zero observation latency.

## What calibration does and does not establish

Quantile regression produces lower, median and upper predictions, but those predictions alone do not certify a desired realized coverage. The system reserves a chronologically later calibration window and computes conformal corrections by horizon bucket. That window remains separate from the earlier fitting targets. Reused calibration summaries are clearly labelled as such and are never substituted for a held-out performance result. The median retains its forecasting role while the interval correction addresses uncertainty around it.

The nominal interval level is 80%. On the published six-month CISO test cohort, demand achieves 77.35% overall realized coverage and passes the predeclared gate. Carbon achieves 71.57% and fails. The coverage figure also shows horizon and local-hour behavior, because an overall average can hide substantial conditional gaps. I kept the failed carbon result in the repository and did not tune the published test period until its score looked acceptable. The next defensible experiment would select recalibration choices on a separate validation cohort and evaluate them on fresh future data.

Conformal theory depends on assumptions that are difficult to preserve in hourly grid series. Observations are dependent, seasonal behavior changes, and unusual weather or generation events can shift the distribution. It would be incorrect to translate a nominal pointwise interval into a guaranteed job-level bound after summing hours. The model card therefore separates nominal interval intent, measured coverage and the scheduler's use of upper endpoints as an operational heuristic. This distinction is especially important because carbon uncertainty already missed its empirical gate.

## Turning forecasts into schedules

The optimizer compares feasible whole-hour allocations under a deadline. It supports contiguous jobs and an interruptible variant, using either the median trajectory or summed upper endpoints as its objective. Jobs are scored on actual future proxy values only after a recommendation has been made. Immediate execution supplies the baseline, while a hindsight optimizer supplies the oracle comparison. Complete actual coverage of the feasible window is required so that the oracle does not benefit from a different set of observable alternatives.

The published replay scores 2,726 scenarios per strategy. Median scheduling achieves 29.37% mean scenario savings relative to immediate execution; the upper strategy achieves 27.35%. The hindsight oracle reaches 33.17%. Nineteen incomplete windows per strategy are excluded and their reason is retained. Longer deadline windows generally offer more flexibility, but the two forecast-based strategies still trail the oracle. These percentages are equally weighted scenario results, not a fleet-level estimate weighted by real arrivals or energy use.

The upper strategy's smaller realized savings are useful evidence rather than an embarrassment to remove. Risk-oriented objectives can trade expected performance for a different ranking of uncertain choices, and unvalidated bounds can be imperfect ranking signals. The project demonstrates that choosing a more conservative-looking objective does not automatically establish a better environmental outcome. A practical deployment would need a workload-specific loss function, transparent uncertainty assumptions and operational constraints beyond the illustrative replay.

## Deployment and deterioration

The offline demonstration packages a bounded real CISO snapshot and selected model artifacts into a FastAPI container. It requires no EIA credential and serves forecasts, scheduling recommendations and metadata under a non-root, read-only runtime. Registry metadata checks target identity, versions, feature contracts, factor definitions and artifact hashes before serving. A broken or incompatible artifact fails closed. Freezing the snapshot makes the demonstration reproducible, but the API notes that its timestamps are historical rather than pretending the demo is live.

Monitoring separates input drift from realized forecast performance. PSI and KS compare a reference window with current observations. Coverage and error checks score only issues at or after the deployed artifact's fit cutoff. The latest eligible forecast per realized target hour is selected, missing observations are counted and unavailable results remain insufficient data. A recently fitted artifact can legitimately have no realized post-fit sample. The monitor does not manufacture a healthy coverage score by replaying that artifact over its own earlier fitting history.

In an isolated experiment, a 30% intensity shift produces a sampled coverage alarm by day seven. Before the shift, realized coverage is 82.14%; at day seven it is 67.09%. Input alarms were already present before injection, and that fact remains in the report. The sampled checkpoints establish a detection interval rather than an hourly delay or a universal seven-day guarantee. Serial dependence, seasonality and multiple comparisons also make the statistical p-values operational diagnostics rather than exact alarm probabilities for this deployment.

## Automation, review and remaining work

Automation code provides scheduled refresh, manual retraining candidates, dependency updates and gated container publication. Retraining reports paired held-out deltas, records revised or excluded labels and opens a draft candidate review without silently changing deployed pointers. Release code reruns the tagged revision's quality gate and verifies a pulled registry digest before updating latest. Successful local and Linux checks establish software behavior; they are not a substitute for live operational acceptance, human model review or complete regional data collection.

At this stage, ERCO evaluation and some live automation checks remain pending. The first dispatched candidate runs stopped at the explicit missing Actions-secret prerequisite, before producing data or model claims. That failure is recorded rather than described as a successful deployment. Future work should establish observation and weather forecast vintages, finish independent regional acceptance, investigate calibration on a new cohort and replay representative workloads with a stronger emissions target. The portfolio's main contribution is an auditable chain from imperfect data to forecasts, decisions and honest limits.
